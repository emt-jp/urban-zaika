## 1. The halal statement — settle this before anything else

A strict Muslim diner's first question is not whether the food is good. It is **"is the kitchen
really halal, and do you serve alcohol?"** Both branches serve alcohol. Every profile and
directory entry currently says nothing about it, and silence loses more customers than an honest
answer: people assume the worst and pick the restaurant that told them.

**Confirmed 19 Sep:** both branches hold **kitchen-level halal certification**. That is the
strongest of the possible positions — it is a claim about the premises, not just the meat supply,
and it is the one halal directories weight most.

Still needed, and it is the last blank:

1. **The certifying body's name and certificate number.** An unnamed certification is treated as
   no certification by strict diners, and Zabihah, HalalNavi and HalalTrip all have a field for
   the issuing authority. Until we can name it, the claim is weaker than the certificate we
   actually hold.
2. **Alcohol** — served to other guests only, or also used in cooking? Kitchen certification
   normally rules out cooking with alcohol, but say it explicitly rather than let people assume.

Published wording, once the body is named — **identical everywhere**, because people cross-check:

> 当店は〈認証機関名〉のハラール認証を取得したキッチンです（両店舗）。肉類はすべてハラール処理されたものを使用し、豚肉は一切取り扱っておりません。
>
> Certified halal kitchen at both branches, certified by 〈body〉. All meat is halal-slaughtered
> and no pork is handled in our kitchen.

Claiming certification that does not exist gets you named in the same community groups you are
trying to win — but under-claiming one you *do* hold, which is what the 十条店 page did until
today, simply gives the credit away.

### There is a certification built for exactly our situation

Japan has **no government halal body and no official national logo** — certification is entirely
private, which is why restaurants display different marks. Relevant here: the **NPO Japan Halal
Association (JHA)** issues a separate **"Muslim-Friendly Restaurant Certification"**, where the
food must meet the same 100% halal standard as full certification **but the restaurant may still
sell alcohol**, provided storage and glassware are kept separate. MPJA runs a comparable
"Muslim Friendly" designation.

That is Urban Zaika's exact position, and it converts our biggest objection into a credential.

- JHA full restaurant certification runs **¥660,000+** new and **¥450,000+** to renew, plus
  auditor travel — steep for two branches. The Muslim-Friendly tier's price is not published;
  the first contact and interview are free, so it costs nothing to ask.
- The process is eight steps: interview, training, pre-inspection, application, document audit,
  on-site audit, decision board, certificate.
- JHA: jhalal.com · MPJA: mpja.jp · NAHA: nipponasia-halal.org (fees unpublished)

**Worth one phone call.** Even if the fee proves too high, the interview will tell you exactly
what the kitchen would need to change, and that answer is what section 1's statement depends on.

### The prayer corner

Two square metres, a clean mat, qibla marked on the wall, and somewhere to make wudu. It costs
almost nothing and it is the single most repeated reason a restaurant gets recommended unprompted
in Muslim food groups in Japan. If the Jujo branch can spare the space, do it there first — it is
the one with longer evening hours.

## 2. These are not one audience

The request was "halal, Nepali, Indian, Pakistani and other South Asian Muslim communities". Two
of those want opposite things, and the copy has to split accordingly.

| Group | Halal a buying trigger? | What actually reaches them |
|---|---|---|
| **Pakistani, Bangladeshi** | Yes — decisive | Urdu/Bengali, mosque networks, Facebook groups, catering |
| **Nepali** | **Mostly no** — the diaspora in Japan is largely Hindu | Nepali language, homestyle value, vegetarian range |
| **Indian** | Mostly no, and beef is a negative | Vegetarian range, no-beef assurance, thali-style value |
| **Indonesian, Malaysian, Turkish, Central Asian, Arab** | Yes | Halal apps and directories, student associations |
| **Japanese Muslim + Muslim-friendly Japanese** | Yes | Japanese-language halal wording on Google and Tabelog |

The Nepali point is worth stating clearly because it is the easiest mistake to make: Nepal's
community in Japan is one of the largest South Asian ones here, and it is **predominantly Hindu,
not Muslim**. Halal messaging does not move them. Nepali-language messaging does. If there are
Nepali staff in either kitchen, that is an asset currently going unused.

Conversely, for Indian Hindu customers — and **Nishikasai, Tokyo's Little India, is in our own
ward** — halal can read as a negative unless paired with a clear vegetarian and no-beef message.

**How to post without getting removed.** Most of these groups ban drive-by advertising. Join,
read for a few days, then post once as a member introducing the restaurant — not as an ad. Offer
the group something (a discount for members), reply to every comment, and do not repost more than
monthly. One well-received post outperforms ten deleted ones.

### Directories — submit in this order

1. **Zabihah** — zabihah.com/add-place, **free**, community-reviewed. Its tags handle our case
   honestly: hand- vs machine-slaughtered, certifying authority, "partial" vs "fully halal menu",

2. **HalalNavi** — business.halal-navi.com/en, **free**, reviewed before publishing.
3. **HalalTrip** — halaltrip.com/restaurant-add (free tier; paid priority listing exists).
4. **Halal Media Japan** — halalmedia.jp/contactus, or Instagram DM.
5. **Halal Gourmet Japan** — halalgourmet.jp/getlisted. **Paid**, with a 3-month trial; they
   interview you and register ingredient data. Worth it only after the free ones are done.

Every one of these asks the same questions: certification status, alcohol policy, prayer space,
photos, menu with prices. Which is why section 1 comes first.

## 4. Catering is the actual opportunity

Delivery platforms take 28–35%. A mosque iftar, a wedding, an aqiqah, a milad, a student Eid
party are ¥30,000–200,000 orders at full margin, booked by phone, from communities that order
this way constantly and complain about the shortage of good options. Two halal Indian-Pakistani
kitchens in Tokyo is a strong position, and we are not visibly offering it anywhere.

The whole first step is one line, added to both Google profiles, the website and every community
post:

> ご宴会・ケータリング承ります（結婚式・アキーカ・イード・モスクの行事など）。お見積りはお電話で。
>
> Catering for weddings, aqiqah, Eid and mosque events — call for a quote.

**Before publishing that line, confirm the kitchen can actually take a 50-person order**, what
the minimum is, and how much notice is needed. Then the mosque committees in section 5 are worth
approaching directly, because they book the same caterer repeatedly once they trust one.

**Cricket** is the cheap companion play. Pakistan, India, Bangladesh and Nepal matches fill a
room. A screening with a set menu costs nothing to run and is exactly what gets shared in these
groups unprompted.

### What to actually say when you walk in

Not a flyer push. At a mosque, ask for the committee, introduce the restaurant, ask whether they
have a caterer for iftar and events, and leave a card. At a halal grocery, ask the owner whether
you can leave flyers and offer to recommend their shop in return — the referral works both ways
and costs nothing. Both conversations go better in the community's own language, which is the
point of section 3.

## 6. Ramadan — February 2027

Iftar sets, sehri packs, and mosque catering. It is the biggest single window of the year for a
halal restaurant, it gets booked weeks ahead, and the restaurants that win it are the ones the
community already knows in January. Everything above is the work that makes February possible.

- **December** — decide iftar set pricing and capacity; approach mosque committees.
- **January** — Google posts, community group posts, flyers in halal groceries.
- **February** — daily iftar service; sehri packs for the 十条店 late hours.

## 7. One housekeeping problem

The Kita-ku branch trades as **SUHANA** on Uber Eats and Rocket Now, **URBAN ZAIQA** on Tabelog,
and **Urban Zaika** on Google. Community traction runs on word of mouth: someone recommends the
restaurant in a group, ten people search the name, and they need to land on us. Right now those
recommendations leak to three different places or to nothing. Pick one name and make every
platform match it.
