# Catering and event stalls

Two businesses, not one. Catering runs out of the kitchens we already license and can start this
week. Stalls need a permit, equipment we do not own, and staff away from the restaurant — and
the 2026 application windows have almost all closed. Sequence them accordingly.

Researched 19 Sep 2026 from ward 保健所 pages, TMG 食品衛生の窓, and organiser sites. Items that
could not be sourced are marked **unverified** rather than guessed; check those by phone before
spending money on them.

---

## The verdict

**Catering: start now.** A 飲食店営業許可 already covers cooking in our own kitchen and delivering
to a venue. No new licence, no fee, no waiting. That makes catering pure margin against a fixed
kitchen cost, and it is the one thing on this page that can earn money in October.

**Stalls: aim at 2027, prepare through the winter.** Nearly every 2026 window has closed. Use the
time to get the standing permit, sort the kit, and be ready when applications open.

## 1. Catering — what the law actually requires

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Do we need a new licence to cater off-site? | **No.** Cooking at the licensed restaurant and delivering to a customer or event is within 飲食店営業. |
| When *would* we need more? | Only if we move to **wholesale** — manufacturing food for other shops to resell, or selling packaged food online. That is そうざい製造業 territory. Direct-to-customer is not. |
| Packaged bento labelling | Food handed over face to face is largely exempt. **Pre-packed bento left for later** needs a full 食品表示法 label: name, ingredients by weight, allergens, use-by (time of day for bento), storage, and producer name and address. |
| Plating at the venue | Gloves and hand sanitising; hold food below 10°C if it is not served straight away. |
| Insurance | **PL保険** (product liability) is not legally required, but food poisoning is the dominant risk in this trade and some organisers ask for proof. Worth quoting before the first large booking. |

**The practical conclusion: nothing is stopping us.** The barriers to catering are sales and
logistics, not regulation.

## 2. Stalls — the two routes

| | 臨時出店届 (notification) | 臨時営業許可 (permit) |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A notification, not a licence | A real mobile/temporary business licence |
| When it applies | Public-interest events only — ward, TMG or residents' association run — and **5 days a year or fewer** | Everything else: commercial events, or more than 5 days a year |
| Fee | None published (**unverified** — confirm with the ward) | **¥4,800** (TMG, from 1 Jun 2024) |
| Validity | Per event | **Five years** — apply once, then work multiple events |

**Get the ¥4,800 permit.** Five years of validity for the price of a couple of catering covers
removes the per-event paperwork gamble entirely, and the notification route's 5-day annual cap
would bind quickly if stalls go well.

Applications go to the **保健所 of the ward hosting the event**, not our home ward.

- **Edogawa-ku** — 03-3658-3177. Submit **7 days before**. Two forms: 行事開催届 (organiser) and
  臨時出店届 (vendor).
- **Kita-ku** — 03-3919-0726. Lead time not published (**unverified**); assume a fortnight.

### The catch that matters most

**Edogawa-ku 保健所 singles out curry rice as requiring mandatory prior consultation**, alongside
noodles, ice cream and mochi, because of past food-poisoning cases. Our main product is on their
watch list. This is not a refusal — it is a phone call — but make that call before committing to
any Edogawa event, not after paying a pitch fee.

Also binding:

- **One food category plus one drink per stall.** Curry *or* kebabs, not both.
- **Banned outright:** raw meat, raw egg, sashimi, fresh cream.
- **Facilities:** running water for handwashing, potable water, waste-water disposal, and
  refrigeration specifically for curry and rice.

## 3. Kit — we own none of it

Budget for a tent, tables, warmers or chafing dishes, handwashing and water containers, a cool
box, a generator if the pitch has no power, and transport. **Rent for the first two events.**
Buying before we know whether stalls convert is how restaurants lose money on this. The pitch fee
is rarely the expensive part.

## 4. What is actually available — and when

### Worth chasing now

| Event | When | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pakistan Japan Friendship Festival** | Early Aug (2026 ran 6–9 Aug) | Ueno Park | The best fit on this list: a confirmed food zone with outside vendors, biryani and karahi already on it, and an open enquiry form at pjftokyo.com. No published deadline — ask now and get on next year's list. |
| **Boishakhi Mela** (Bengali New Year) | Mid-April | Ikebukuro Nishiguchi Park, Toshima | Bengali New Year with a curry festival attached, run by the Japan Bangladesh Society — and the Jujo branch is already inside Tokyo's largest Bangladeshi community. Vendor process unverified; ask. |
| **Nepal Festival** | July | Odaiba (venue rotates) | Around 40 stalls including food, run with NRNA Japan. |

### Closed for 2026, diary for 2027

Kita-ku 区民まつり (early Oct, applications closed 17 Jul), Edogawa-ku 区民まつり (mid-Oct, closed
29 Aug), Itabashi (closed 31 Jul — and it **requires ward residency or a workplace in Itabashi**,
which probably rules us out). These windows open roughly eleven weeks ahead, so **watch from
July 2027** and put a reminder in now.

### Not worth it

- **FOODEX 2027** — ¥550,000 a booth. No.
- **Japan Halal Expo**, Makuhari, 25–27 Nov 2026 — $2,700–$4,900. It is a B2B trade show, not a
  consumer event; it sells to distributors, not diners. Skip unless we want wholesale customers.
- **University festivals** — Rikkyo, Tokyo University of Science, Toyo Akabanedai and the rest all
  appear to restrict stalls to student societies. No outside-vendor track was found at any campus
  near either branch.
- **Embassy national days** — invitation-only receptions across all six countries, with no
  evidence of outside food vendors. The public festivals above are the way in, not the receptions.

already have, and Tokyo offices book it in October. A halal, vegetarian-friendly option is a
genuine differentiator for a team that currently avoids booking anywhere because one colleague
cannot eat what is served.
